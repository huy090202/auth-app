How a step-up works

Three calls. The browser only ever talks to your backend.

1

Open a challenge

Your backend names the user, the action it is about to allow, and the factor. For a passkey, AuthGate returns the WebAuthn options.

2

The user answers

Your page collects the PIN or runs the passkey ceremony, and your backend passes the answer on.

3

Check the token

Verify the signature against the public keys, then aud, exp and purpose. A token for one action never unlocks another.

What you don't have to build

The parts of 2FA that are easy to get wrong.

PINs

Hashed with argon2id, a length policy, trivial PINs refused, lockout after repeated failures.

Passkeys

WebAuthn options and verification, checked against the domain and origins you registered.

Signed tokens

ES256, signed in AWS KMS, 5 minutes by default. Verify offline with the JWKS.

Keys and limits

Rotate a client key with a 24-hour grace, end it early, and a rate limit per client.

Self-service

Sign up, confirm your email, add a second factor, and create clients on the Portal.

Serverless

Go on AWS Lambda behind CloudFront, DynamoDB, KMS. No servers to run.
